Three beers from one wort: explore the GC-MS peaks

Lark Hill Brewery × University of Salford · Black Cat, Liverpool · 3 December 2026

Lark Hill split one batch of wort between three fermenters. #4 Separate From Everything got Pomona yeast and a Citra and Mosaic dry hop, #5 Heads In Your Hats got Munich Classic wheat-beer yeast and an Amarillo dry hop, and #6 Like A Heartbeat got Philly Sour yeast and cranberry purée.

How to read this

What GC-MS does

The beer's aroma molecules were pulled out with a solvent and injected into a gas chromatograph. Molecules travel through a very thin column about 30 m long at different speeds, so they come out one after another; the time each one takes is its retention time. A mass spectrometer then breaks each molecule into fragments and identifies it by matching the fragment pattern against a reference library.

Two solvents and two runs

Each beer was extracted with dichloromethane (DCM) and, separately, with ethyl acetate (EtOAc). Each solvent picks up a slightly different set of molecules. Every extract was run twice; we only count a molecule as found if it shows up in both runs.

What the numbers mean

Peak size (area) tells you how much of a molecule there is relative to the other beers, within the same solvent. It is not a concentration, because no reference compound of known strength was added. Bigger peaks are not always stronger smells: some molecules are smelled at tiny amounts, others hardly at all.

The library match

Each peak's name comes with a match score out of 100. Above about 85 is a confident match. Many small peaks have low scores and uncertain names; this explorer hides peaks scoring below 70. Some peaks, such as oleamide, also appear in the solvent blank and are background, not beer.
